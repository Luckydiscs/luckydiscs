Two Money Shots (4 / 3 / 1 / 3) from the same mould. One costs €12.90, the other €14.90. When they are new you can barely tell them apart. After a season, you can.

Plastic is the part of disc selection beginners skip and experienced players think about longest. Flight numbers describe the mould. Plastic decides how the disc feels in your hand, how it behaves in the rain, and what it turns into after a hundred rounds. Here is a look at the three Lucky Discs plastics, including the cases where the cheaper one is better.

## What plastic changes

Flight numbers belong to the mould, but plastic affects how the disc actually flies and how long it stays that way. The difference comes down to three things.

**Wear.** Every tree and rock hit reshapes the rim and dome slightly. Soft plastic changes quickly, hard plastic slowly. As a disc wears it becomes more understable, which means it turns right more easily than it did when new.

**Grip.** The surface and softness of the plastic decide how well the disc stays in your fingers when it is cold or damp.

**Shape out of the mould.** Different plastics settle in the mould slightly differently, so discs from the same mould can fly a little differently even when new. That is why it pays to throw a disc yourself rather than rely on the numbers alone.

## Basic: cheapest, grippy and made to wear

Basic is the base plastic: softer, matte and the most affordable. In our range the Money Shot comes in Basic at €12.90, two euros less than Premium.

Its strength is grip. When your fingers are cold or the disc is damp, soft base plastic stays in your hand better than hard, smooth plastic. On approach shots and putts that is a real advantage, which is why many players throw drivers in durable plastic but approach with base plastic.

Its weakness is durability. A Basic disc takes tree hits badly: the rim flattens, the dome relaxes and the disc starts turning right more easily. Not everyone sees that as a flaw. Plenty of players beat a disc in on purpose until it flies exactly the line they want.

Basic suits:

- beginners who want a secure grip
- approach and putting discs
- players who want to beat a disc in to their own taste

## Premium: the default for discs you actually play

Premium is clearly more durable than base plastic, and it is what most players use for most of their throws. In Premium you can get the Money Shot, the Bank Robber (8 / 5 / -1 / 2) and the Treasure Hunt (12 / 6 / -1 / 3), all at €14.90.

The value of Premium is predictability. When a disc wears slowly, its flight stays the same season after season. It is hard to refine a throw if the disc keeps changing underneath you.

When new, harder plastic can feel slicker than base plastic. That settles once the surface dulls with use.

Premium suits:

- wooded fairways where discs hit trees
- fairway and distance drivers
- players who want a disc to fly the same next summer as it does now

## Ultrium: when a disc must not change

Ultrium is the top plastic in our range. Its job is to keep a disc the same for as long as possible while still giving a good grip. In Ultrium you can get the Money Shot, Bank Robber and Treasure Hunt at €14.90, and the Jailbreak (5 / 4 / 0 / 2) at €21.90.

The practical difference from Premium is time. If you have found a disc whose flight you trust blind, Ultrium is how you keep it that way for several seasons.

Ultrium suits:

- rocky, rough courses where discs wear fastest
- tournament discs
- the disc that takes half of your throws

## Plastic and weather

**Freezing temperatures.** All plastic stiffens in the cold. Base plastic holds its grip best, but it is also the most likely to crack in hard frost or on impact. Take discs you would not mind cracking on a winter round, and keep the one you are about to throw warm in your pocket. Our [winter disc golf guide](/blog/winter-disc-golf-play-year-round) has more on playing in the cold.

**Rain and morning dew.** A wet surface is slippery whatever the plastic, but soft base plastic loses the least grip. A towel on your bag helps more than any plastic choice.

**Heat.** A disc left in a hot car or in direct sun can change shape permanently. Keep your discs in the shade.

## How to choose in two minutes

1. **Approach and putting disc:** Basic if you value grip, Premium if the disc often ends up in trees. The Money Shot comes in both, so the comparison is cheap to run.
2. **Fairway and distance drivers:** Premium by default. These take the most tree and rock hits, and their flight is the one you want to keep unchanged.
3. **The disc you trust most:** Ultrium. If one disc takes half of your throws, buy it in the plastic that lasts.

The two-euro gap between Basic and Premium is small compared with how many rounds a disc gets thrown. Upgrading is still not automatically right: a base-plastic approach disc can be the best disc in your bag precisely because it stays in your hand.

## Summary

Basic gives grip and wears quickly. Premium is the predictable default for wooded courses. Ultrium keeps a disc the same for longest.

The easiest way to learn the difference is to buy the same mould in two plastics and throw them back to back from the same tee. You will hardly notice it on the first throw, but you will by the tenth round.
