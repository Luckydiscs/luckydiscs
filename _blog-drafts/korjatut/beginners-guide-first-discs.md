Disc golf is easy to pick up: all you need is a disc and the nearest course. Even so, a lot of beginners make the same mistake. They buy a bagful of fast drivers straight away, the kind that are nearly impossible to throw straight. Three well-chosen discs will take you further than ten of the wrong kind.

## Start with three discs

In our view, three discs are enough for a beginner: a putter, a midrange and one fairway driver you can control. With only a few discs you learn each flight properly instead of guessing your way through ten different ones.

## Putter: accuracy near the basket

The putter is your most important disc. You finish holes with it, and it is how you learn clean technique. A slow putter flies predictably and forgives small mistakes. The Lucky Discs putt-and-approach disc is the Money Shot (4 / 3 / 1 / 3). Turn +1 and fade 3 mean it will not turn right but reliably finishes to the left, so it holds its line in wind and on firmer throws. Pick a plastic that feels good in your hand and practise putting in the garden. It is the quickest way to bring your scores down.

## Midrange: the most versatile tool

The midrange is the disc you will play most holes with early on. It flies straight, is easy to control and gives you enough distance without a powerful throw. The Lucky Discs Jailbreak (5 / 4 / 0 / 2) is a straight-flying midrange: turn 0 means it barely turns early in the flight, and fade 2 brings it down in a controlled way at the end. The Jailbreak is made from durable Ultrium plastic, which holds its flight characteristics for a long time.

## Fairway driver: distance you can control

Once you have the basics down, a fairway driver adds distance without making the flight unpredictable. The Lucky Discs Bank Robber (8 / 5 / -1 / 2) is a moderate-speed fairway driver with a reliable flight, even on technical holes. It is clearly easier to throw than a true distance driver, so it will serve you well for a long time.

## Don't start with the fastest drivers

Distance drivers like the Lucky Discs Treasure Hunt (12 / 6 / -1 / 3) are tempting, because a strong arm can throw them a long way. A beginner's throw doesn't give them enough speed, so they don't fly as designed: they fade left early and land short. Save the distance driver for when your technique and power have come along. That is when it really pays off.

## Weight and plastic

A lighter disc gets going with less power and often suits a beginner. Plastic affects durability and feel: tough plastic shrugs off hits against trees and keeps its flight for longer. Don't worry about this too much at the start. The main thing is to get out and throw.

## Summary

A putter, a midrange and one controllable fairway driver. With that combination you will learn the basics faster and stay on the fairway more often than with a full bag of fast discs.
